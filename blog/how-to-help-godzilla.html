How to Help Godzilla: A Practical Guide for Concerned Citizens

JUN 5, 2026 · By Community Outreach Division

Many citizens write to the Bureau asking what they can do to help. The most common letter begins with a variation of 'I'm not a scientist or a lawyer, but I want to do something.' The Bureau's answer is always the same: you don't need specialized expertise to make a difference. The campaign to reclassify Godzilla as a Protected Ecological Asset is a democratic project — it depends on ordinary citizens taking actions that, individually, seem small, but collectively, create the conditions for change. This guide presents five actions ranked by impact, based on the Bureau's assessment of what actually moves the needle.

1. Sign the Petition. The petition to reclassify Godzilla as a Protected Ecological Asset is the single most effective action any individual can take. Each signature brings us closer to 1,000 — our Phase 1 goal — and ultimately to the 100,000 required for UN consideration. The petition is not merely a symbolic gesture — it is a formal legal document that, when it reaches the threshold, triggers a mandatory review by the UN Committee on Kaiju Affairs. The Bureau has prepared the accompanying legal brief, assembled the supporting evidence, and organized the coalition of supporting organizations. What we need is the number. Your signature is a number. Sign at savegodzilla.org/petition.html. It takes 90 seconds.

2. Educate Yourself and Others. The most powerful force working against Godzilla's reclassification is not military opposition or political resistance — it is simple ignorance. Most people have never considered the question of Godzilla's legal status. Most people assume that Godzilla is classified as a threat because he is a threat. Most people have never seen the incident reports that tell a different story. Read the incident reports at savegodzilla.org/incidents.html. Read the Bureau's blog at savegodzilla.org/blog.html. Understand the difference between an 'attack' and a 'protective intervention.' Then share what you learn — with your family, your friends, your coworkers, your social networks. The Bureau has prepared a one-page summary of the key facts (Form GZ-15) that is designed to be shared. Print it. Forward it. Pin it on a bulletin board.

3. Attend a Public Hearing. Public hearings are held every Thursday evening at 7 PM in the Community Room of the Municipal Annex. The Bureau recognizes that most citizens cannot attend every hearing, and many citizens do not live within commuting distance. If you can attend even one hearing, your presence matters. The public comment period is your opportunity to directly address the Director of Kaiju Affairs and the review committee. Your comment is entered into the official record and may influence committee deliberations. If you cannot attend in person, written comments may be submitted via email to hello@savegodzilla.org and are given the same weight as oral comments. Hearing schedules and agendas are posted at savegodzilla.org/contact.html.

4. Share Your Certificate. When you sign the petition, you receive a Certificate of Public Participation — a printable document bearing your name, your certificate number, and your signature number. This certificate is not merely a souvenir. It is a conversation starter. Share it with your friends. Post it in your workspace. Include it in your email signature. Every person who sees it learns two things: that Godzilla lacks legal standing, and that there is something they can do about it. The certificate is designed to be shared — it includes the Bureau's website and a brief explanation of the petition. The more certificates are visible in the world, the more people will learn about Godzilla's legal status.

5. Support the Bureau. The Municipal Bureau of Kaiju Affairs is entirely funded by individual supporters. We accept no government or military funding. Your contribution — in any amount — goes directly to incident reclassification, legal research, public education, and the maintenance of our petition infrastructure. A $5 donation funds one day of incident reclassification research. A $25 donation publishes one corrected incident report in the public record. A $100 donation provides legal research for one amicus brief filing. Every dollar has a specific, measurable impact. Support the Bureau at savegodzilla.org/donate.html. The Bureau operates on a budget of approximately $200,000 per year — less than the cost of a single Maser Cannon discharge. Your support matters.
